Background

About

I am a student in Bengaluru. Before this I spent time in customer support operations, which turned out to be an unusually good way to learn what data is actually for. Working complaint queues teaches you that a metric is a promise someone is accountable for, that an average hides the cases that actually hurt, and that the number on the dashboard is only as good as the definition nobody wrote down.

That shows up in how I build. Every one of these projects starts by asking what would make the result checkable — and several of them exist because the checkable version disagreed with the obvious one.

I try to follow the whole path rather than stopping at the interesting part: designing the data, modelling the warehouse, building the pipeline, training and calibrating the model, testing it adversarially, and building the interface someone would actually use. Most of what I have learned came from the last two steps.

What seventeen projects taught me

None of these needed more data, a better model or more compute. Each cost me an afternoon, and each one changed what I was entitled to claim. I learned every one of them by getting it wrong first.
